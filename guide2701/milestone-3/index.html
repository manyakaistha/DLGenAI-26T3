Milestone 3 Student Study Guides

Transfer Learning & Stronger Segmentation Models (4 November 2026)

Welcome to Milestone 3 of the Industrial Surface Anomaly Segmentation curriculum!

In Milestone 1, you inspected the physical and statistical characteristics of industrial line-scan sensors: unraveling 1-indexed Fortran-order Run-Length Encodings (RLE), analyzing extreme anomaly sparsity across steel strips, and examining the metric penalties imposed by empty, defect-free material.

In Milestone 2, you constructed your end-to-end baseline systems: establishing clean PyTorch Dataset contracts, implementing industrial geometric and photometric augmentations, setting up combination-stratified cross-validation splits, logging experiments to Weights & Biases, training a baseline from-scratch U-Net, and calibrating post-processing decision pipelines.

In Milestone 3, you elevate your modeling capability from simple baseline networks to state-of-the-art transfer learning architectures: adapting pretrained vision backbones to single-channel industrial strips, contrasting independent multi-label sigmoid heads against mutually exclusive multi-class softmax partitions, mastering compound loss formulations (BCE + Soft Dice), and conducting rigorous, controlled ablation experiments.

1. Prerequisites from Milestones 1 and 2

Before beginning Milestone 3, ensure you are comfortable with these core concepts established in earlier milestones:

  1. Industrial Aspect Ratio Awareness: Line-scan sensor images are \(128 \times 800\) pixels (anisotropic \(6.25:1\) ratio). You understand why square resizing (\(256 \times 256\)) distorts thin longitudinal defects and why native resolutions must be preserved.
  2. PyTorch Tensor Contracts: You understand the standard segmentation tensor conventions: image inputs of shape [B, 1, 128, 800] and multi-label target masks of shape [B, 4, 128, 800].
  3. Combination Stratification & Leakage Isolation: You know how to group rare defect occurrences into multi-label combination signatures to guarantee stable representation across validation folds without data leakage.
  4. The Decision Pipeline: You understand how raw continuous logits map to thresholded binary masks (\(\tau\)) and how connected-component minimum area filtering (\(A_{\min}\)) suppresses high-frequency sensor noise.
  5. RLE Submission Format: You know how to encode binary masks into 1-indexed column-major Fortran strings with the mandatory single-space sentinel (" ") for empty predictions.

2. Curriculum Map: Milestone 3 Topics & Study Guides

The Milestone 3 curriculum is organized into six focused, progressive learning modules:

Milestone 3 Core Topic Primary Study Guide Theoretical Principles Practical Implementation Focus
1. Encoder–Decoder Architectures Guide 01: Encoder–Decoder Architectures Contracting path, bottleneck, expanding path, receptive fields, skip connections (concatenation vs. addition), spatial dimension alignment on \(128 \times 800\). Trace feature tensor shapes through progressive downsampling stages; diagnose spatial phase distortion bugs.
2. Pretrained Backbones Guide 02: Pretrained Backbones & Transfer Learning ImageNet visual feature hierarchies, ResNet residual highways vs. EfficientNet compound MBConv scaling, multi-scale feature taps, freezing vs. fine-tuning, BatchNorm drift. Extract multi-scale feature hierarchies from standard backbones; configure decoders to consume heterogeneous stage channels.
3. Grayscale Input Adaptation Guide 03: Grayscale Input Adaptation Single-channel sensor physics, 3-channel RGB impedance mismatch, 3x channel repeat, first-layer conv weight collapse (sum vs. average), learned \(1\times 1\) adapter. Implement and numerically verify activation equivalence across the 3 grayscale adaptation strategies on CPU.
4. Output Heads & Target Semantics Guide 04: Output Heads & Target Semantics 4-channel independent Sigmoid vs. 5-channel mutually exclusive Softmax, target representations, mathematical activations, empirical mask overlap audit (\(\sum m_i \cdot m_j\)). Audit ground truth masks for pixel-level overlap before choosing a target format; resolve test-time multi-channel collisions.
5. Loss Formulations Guide 05: BCE, Dice, & Compound Losses Extreme class imbalance, numerically stable BCE with logits, differentiable Soft Dice loss, smoothing factor \(\epsilon\), compound \(\text{BCE} + \lambda \text{Dice}\), loss vs. metric separation. Compare BCE and Soft Dice responses on tiny \(4\times 4\) synthetic masks under missed defects, false alarms, and clean sheets.
6. Controlled Experimentation Guide 06: Systematic Model Exploration Scientific single-variable ablation, head and loss compatibility matrix, training trajectory triage (overfitting, erosion, collapse), debugging protocols. Design and execute a 3-phase controlled exploration matrix holding splits, budgets, and post-processing strictly constant.

3. Guide Overviews & Learning Sequence

Follow this recommended reading and implementation sequence:

  • Guide 01: Encoder–Decoder Architectures for Industrial Segmentation: Explains why standard classification features cannot output pixel-aligned masks. Traces a fictional \(128 \times 800\) strip through a 4-stage contracting path, bottleneck, and expanding path. Dissects skip connections (concatenation vs. addition), receptive field expansion, and why odd dimensions cause fatal shape errors.

  • Guide 02: Pretrained Backbones & Transfer Learning: ResNet to EfficientNet: Explores what pretrained backbones learn on ImageNet and why low-level edge filters transfer seamlessly to industrial anomaly detection. Compares the architectural building blocks of ResNet (residual shortcuts) and EfficientNet (MBConv, depthwise convolutions, SE attention), and details fine-tuning dynamics and BatchNorm stability.

  • Guide 03: Adapting Pretrained RGB Backbones to Single-Channel Grayscale: Tackles the single-channel sensor dilemma. Works through three defensible adaptation strategies: 3-channel repetition, first-layer convolution weight collapse (sum vs. average), and learned \(1\times 1\) adapters. Proves exact numerical activation equivalence and analyzes normalization physics.

  • Guide 04: Output Heads & Target Semantics: Multi-Label Sigmoid vs. Multi-Class Softmax: Contrasts 4-channel independent sigmoid multilabel detection with 5-channel mutually exclusive softmax partitions. Works through a concrete numerical demonstration of a competing-defect pixel, details the step-by-step empirical mask overlap audit, and analyzes permanent information loss when forcing overlapping annotations into integers.

  • Guide 05: Segmentation Loss Functions: BCE, Dice, & Compound Objectives: Addresses extreme class imbalance where \(97\%+\) of pixels are clean background. Explains BCEWithLogitsLoss, derives the differentiable Soft Dice loss with smoothing \(\epsilon\), shows how each loss behaves on small missed defects and false alarm speckles, and separates continuous training loss from discrete evaluation metrics.

  • Guide 06: Systematic Model Exploration & Controlled Experimentation: Establishes the single-variable ablation protocol to avoid "alchemy" in deep learning. Details the Milestone 3 Exploration Matrix (Backbone \(\to\) Adaptation \(\to\) Head/Loss), maps compatible head-loss pairings, and provides a diagnostic guide to triage training failure modes from learning curves.

To support hands-on learning without disclosing competition training results, this curriculum includes complete, self-contained educational resources built entirely on synthetic data:

  • Grayscale Adaptation Equivalence: examples/toy_grayscale_adaptation.py Numerically proves that first-layer weight summing produces exact mathematical parity with 3-channel input repetition.

  • Output Head Semantics & Competing Logits: examples/toy_head_comparison.py Calculates independent Sigmoid vs. mutually exclusive Softmax probabilities and decision outcomes on synthetic competing logits.

  • BCE vs. Soft Dice Loss Behavior: examples/toy_loss_behavior.py Evaluates BCE and Soft Dice responses on tiny \(4\times 4\) synthetic masks under missed defects, false alarms, and clean empty sheets.

5. Teaching Philosophy and Boundaries

These study guides are designed as a guiding hand, not a shortcut:

  • They explain the underlying mathematics, tensor dimensions, software interfaces, failure modes, and debugging techniques.
  • They do not provide a copy-paste competition training script, pre-computed hyperparameter values, or trained model checkpoints.
  • They do not reveal confidential exploratory data analysis findings or performance benchmarks from the private training set.
  • You must write, audit, and debug your own segmentation pipeline against data/public/.
