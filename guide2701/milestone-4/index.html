Milestone 4 Student Study Guides

Training Improvements & Model Optimization (11 November 2026)

Welcome to Milestone 4 of the Industrial Surface Anomaly Segmentation project!

In Milestone 1, you inspected the physical line-scan sensor domain, Fortran-order RLE encodings, and the metric penalty on empty masks. In Milestone 2, you constructed your end-to-end segmentation pipeline: PyTorch datasets, industrial augmentations, combination-stratified splits, baseline U-Net models, post-processing filters, and generated your first valid Kaggle submission.

In Milestone 4, you advance from building a functional baseline to mastering optimization, training dynamics, numerical efficiency, and scientific error diagnostics. You will learn how to stabilize training on rare defects, manage GPU memory with mixed precision, schedule learning rates, govern early stopping, and decompose validation errors to make principled engineering improvements.

1. Prerequisites from Earlier Milestones

Before diving into Milestone 4, ensure you are comfortable with these core foundations established in Milestones 1 and 2:

  1. Aspect Ratio and Strip Geometry: Industrial line-scan images are \(800 \times 128\) pixels (\(6.25:1\) ratio). Native dimensions must be preserved to avoid distorting longitudinal defects.
  2. Multi-Label Mask Representation: You understand that targets are 4-channel binary tensors [4, 128, 800] representing classes defect_a, defect_b, defect_c, and defect_d.
  3. Combination Stratification: You understand why naive random splitting causes rare defects (like defect_b) to suffer extreme fold variance, and why image-level combination stratification is mandatory to prevent data leakage.
  4. Baseline U-Net Mechanics: You understand the encoder contracting path, decoder expanding path, lateral skip connections, and compound BCE + Soft Dice loss functions.
  5. Post-Processing Pipeline: You know how continuous logits are converted into binary masks via decision thresholds \(\tau\), and how connected-component analysis applies minimum area filtering (\(A_{\min}\)).

2. Curriculum Map: Milestone 4 Topics & Study Guides

The five core Milestone 4 curriculum requirements are organized into seven focused, progressive study guides:

1. Class Imbalance Guide 01: Multi-Level Class Imbalance Image-level vs. class-level vs. pixel-level imbalance; data oversampling vs. loss weighting; prior shift; evaluation integrity. Implement bounded square-root WeightedRandomSampler and class-balanced Dice; monitor rare-class recall vs. clean-image false alarms.
2. Learning-Rate Schedulers Guide 02: LR Schedulers & Warmup Optimization landscape; linear warmup stabilization; Step, Cosine Annealing, and Plateau decay; per-batch vs. per-epoch stepping. Implement LambdaLR with warmup and cosine decay; diagnose early gradient shattering and late-stage oscillation.
3. Early Stopping & Checkpoints Guide 03: Early Stopping & Governance Monitored quantity selection; minimum delta \(\delta_{\min}\); patience window; atomic checkpointing; holdout overfitting risk. Build an EarlyStopping tracker with automatic rollback to best.pt; evaluate 5-fold OOF cross-validation to prevent validation drift.

3. Guide Overviews & Recommended Reading Order

Follow this recommended reading and implementation sequence:

  • Guide 01: Multi-Level Class Imbalance: Image, Class, and Pixel Mechanics: Deconstructs imbalance into image-level (clean material), class-level (rare defect_b), and pixel-level (0.2% defect canvas occupancy). Explains why aggressive oversampling shifts the model's prior and floods clean images with false alarms. Walks through a 100-strip numerical example of bounded square-root sampling and proves why validation data must remain strictly unweighted.

  • Guide 02: Learning-Rate Schedulers and Warmup Strategies: Explores how learning-rate dynamics dictate optimization traversal. Demonstrates why randomly initialized decoders require linear warmup to prevent gradient shattering of pretrained backbones. Compares Step Decay, Cosine Annealing, and ReduceLROnPlateau, and resolves the per-batch versus per-epoch stepping cadence trap.

  • Guide 03: Early Stopping, Checkpoint Governance, and Validation Drift: Investigates why validation loss minimums often diverge from peak Foreground Dice. Details the mechanics of patience, min_delta, and atomic checkpoint serialization. Warns against the hazard of holdout overfitting (validation drift) caused by repeated tuning against the same validation fold.

  • Guide 04: Mixed Precision (AMP) and GPU Memory Optimization: Provides a forensic breakdown of GPU VRAM (weights, gradients, AdamW moments, and activation tensors). Explains how PyTorch 2.x torch.amp leverages Tensor Cores in FP16/BF16, details dynamic gradient scaling (GradScaler) to prevent gradient underflow, and demonstrates gradient accumulation to decouple hardware microbatch sizes from effective optimization batches.

  • Guide 05: Metric Governance: Validation Dice, Empty Pairs, and Overfitting: Exposes the "Naive Dice Trap"—proving how a lazy model that predicts all zeros achieves an 85% naive Dice score. Dissects the 2×2 contingency matrix of image-class pairs and explains the official 70/20/10 competition score. Categorizes six learning curve archetypes to diagnose overfitting, underfitting, and probability drift.

  • Guide 06: Validation Error Analysis and Spatial Defect Diagnostics: Establishes a 4-view visual audit workflow and defines the six spatial error archetypes (missed thin defects, false alarms on clean material, class confusion, speckle noise, boundary dilation, and fragmented cracks). Introduces defect size binning and stratified sampling to prevent the cherry-picking trap.

  • Guide 07: Controlled Experimentation and Scientific Iteration: Synthesizes optimization into the scientific method. Enforces the One-Factor-At-A-Time (OFAT) rule, details invariant baseline anchors, structures an experiment audit matrix, and explains how ablation studies identify dead code in your pipeline.

4. Standalone Code Examples & Synthetic Walkthrough

To support hands-on learning without disclosing confidential competition benchmarks, this curriculum includes complete, self-contained educational resources built entirely on synthetic data:

1. Interactive Walkthrough Notebook

  • Jupyter Notebook: notebooks/synthetic_optimization_walkthrough.ipynb
  • Paired Python Script: notebooks/synthetic_optimization_walkthrough.py
  • Setup Guide: notebooks/README.md

This walkthrough runs on CPU in under 3 seconds and interactively demonstrates bounded sampling, warmup/cosine scheduling, early stopping, PyTorch 2.x AMP accumulation, multi-metric decomposition, and spatial error binning.

2. Standalone Diagnostic Scripts

  • Imbalance & Bounded Sampler: examples/toy_imbalance_sampler.py Simulates a 200-strip imbalanced dataset, compares natural uniform draws against bounded weighted draws, and audits class exposure over 10 epochs.

  • Learning Rate Scheduler Simulator: examples/toy_scheduler_visualizer.py Simulates and tabulates learning-rate trajectories across Warmup + Cosine, StepLR, and ReduceLROnPlateau.

  • Early Stopping & Checkpoint Rollback: examples/toy_early_stopping.py Executes an early stopping tracker across 20 validation epochs, demonstrating patience counting, noise filtering (min_delta), and automatic weight restoration.

  • PyTorch 2.x AMP Lifecycle: examples/toy_amp_lifecycle.py Demonstrates torch.amp.autocast, torch.amp.GradScaler, microbatch accumulation, unscaling, and gradient norm clipping on toy tensors.

  • Validation Error Analyzer: examples/toy_error_analyzer.py Decomposes validation predictions into presence recall, overlap recall, clean-image specificity, conditional Dice, and defect size bins.

5. Teaching Philosophy and Boundaries

These study guides are designed as a scientific guide, not a hyperparameter cheat sheet:

  • They explain the underlying mathematics, numerical mechanics, failure modes, and debugging protocols.
  • They do not provide pre-computed hyperparameter values, optimal batch sizes, private class weights, or pre-trained checkpoints.
  • They do not reveal internal exploratory data analysis findings or competition benchmarks from the private training set.
  • There are no assessment questions or graded tasks in this folder; all exercises are clearly marked ungraded activities.
  • You must formulate your own hypotheses, conduct your own controlled experiments, and audit your models against data/public/.
