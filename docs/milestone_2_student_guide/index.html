Milestone 2 Student Study Guides

Building the Segmentation Pipeline & First Submission

Welcome to Milestone 2 of the Industrial Surface Anomaly Segmentation project!

In Milestone 1, you inspected raw annotations, unraveled Run-Length Encoding (RLE) memory layouts, examined the metric penalty on empty masks, and explored the physical constraints of line-scan imaging sensors. In Milestone 2, you move from analytical observation to active systems engineering: designing, training, and validating your first complete deep learning segmentation pipeline, and generating a valid Kaggle submission file.

1. Prerequisites from Milestone 1

Before embarking on Milestone 2, ensure you are comfortable with these core concepts established in Milestone 1:

  1. Aspect Ratio Awareness: Industrial line-scan images are \(800 \times 128\) pixels (\(6.25:1\) ratio). You understand why square resizing (\(256 \times 256\)) distorts thin longitudinal defects and why native resolutions must be preserved.
  2. Fortran-Order RLE Traversal: You understand that RLE strings are 1-indexed and traverse columns from top to bottom (order='F'), requiring index clamping at \(102,400\) pixels.
  3. The Multi-Label Long-Format Schema: You understand that train.csv provides four rows per image (one per defect class) and that empty masks represent clean, defect-free material.
  4. Metric Sensitivity: You understand the mathematical formulation of the Dice coefficient (\(F_1\) score) and the official 70/20/10 weighted competition metric.

2. Curriculum Map: Milestone 2 Topics & Study Guides

The six core Milestone 2 competition requirements are organized into eight focused, progressive study guides:

Milestone 2 Core Topic Primary Study Guide(s) Theoretical Principles Practical Implementation Focus
1. Dataset & DataLoader Guide 01: The Dataset Contract
Guide 02: Batching & DataLoaders		 Industrial line-scan geometry, RLE decoding mechanics, tensor contracts [C,H,W], multi-process IPC, pinned memory. Build a clean PyTorch Dataset and DataLoader yielding image [B, 1, 128, 800] and mask [B, 4, 128, 800].
2. Industrial Augmentations Guide 03: Industrial Augmentations Domain invariances on moving strip, geometric vs photometric duality, mask discreteness, nearest-neighbor interpolation. Implement synchronized horizontal/vertical flips and image-only contrast jitter; diagnose spatial misalignment bugs.
3. Validation Split Guide 04: Multi-Label Stratification The rare-defect "split lottery", hypergeometric sampling variance, combination signatures, data leakage vectors. Create a combination-stratified \(80/20\) image-level holdout that guarantees support for rare classes like defect_b.
4. W&B Experiment Tracking Guide 05: W&B Experiment Tracking The experiment lifecycle, step-axis synchronization, qualitative mask overlays, model artifact lineage, API security. Configure a reproducible W&B logging workflow tracking training loss, validation Dice, and model checkpoints.
5. Baseline U-Net & Training Guide 06: Baseline U-Net & Training Encoder-decoder contracting/expanding paths, skip connections, BCE + Soft Dice compound loss, optimization lifecycle. Implement and train a baseline U-Net for 5–10 epochs; monitor loss convergence and save the best checkpoint.
6. Prediction, RLE & Submission Guide 07: Logits to Binary Masks
Guide 08: RLE & First Submission		 Probability thresholding \(\tau\), 8-connected components, minimum area filtering \(A_{\min}\), Fortran RLE, single-space sentinel. Calibrate post-processing thresholds, encode binary masks to RLE strings, and generate a 12,001-row submission CSV.

3. Guide Overviews & Learning Sequence

Follow this recommended reading and implementation sequence:

  • Guide 01: The Dataset Contract: From Annotation Rows to Aligned Strip Tensors: Traces fictional image STRIP_0042 from raw JPEG and CSV rows through grayscale loading, Fortran RLE decoding, boundary clamping, and 4-channel mask stacking. Teaches the empirical mask overlap audit.

  • Guide 02: Batching, DataLoaders, and Image-Level Isolation: Explains how PyTorch's DataLoader collates tensors into 4D minibatches [B, C, H, W], details worker seeding with worker_init_fn, and proves why splitting must occur strictly at the image ID level to prevent catastrophic row-level leakage.

  • Guide 03: Industrial Augmentations: Geometric Alignment and Photometric Invariance: Separates geometric transforms (applied to image and mask identically) from photometric transforms (applied to image only). Discusses the physical plausibility of horizontal vs. vertical flips on steel strips and demonstrates why masks require nearest-neighbor interpolation.

  • Guide 04: Validation Strategy: Multi-Label Stratification for Rare Defect Detection: Demonstrates how naive random splits cause severe variance for rare defects (like defect_b), introduces multi-label combination stratification, and provides a strict data leakage prevention checklist.

  • Guide 05: Experiment Tracking with Weights & Biases: Walks through the complete W&B experiment lifecycle: configuration logging, step axis synchronization, interactive qualitative mask overlays, model artifact versioning, and secure API key management.

  • Guide 06: Baseline Segmentation with U-Net: Architecture, Heads, and Optimization: Dissects U-Net's contracting path, bottleneck, expanding path, and skip connections. Traces feature map shapes for a \(128 \times 800\) strip, contrasts 4-Sigmoid vs. 5-Softmax heads, details the BCE + Soft Dice compound loss, and guides a modest 5-epoch sanity run.

  • Guide 07: Post-Processing & The Decision Pipeline: Logits to Binary Masks: Explains how to bridge continuous model logits to binary masks via decision thresholds \(\tau\), analyzes connected components with OpenCV, and applies minimum area filtering (\(A_{\min}\)) to suppress sensor noise speckles.

  • Guide 08: Run-Length Encoding and First Kaggle Submission: Details the 1-indexed column-major Fortran RLE encoding algorithm, explains the mandatory single-space sentinel (" ") for empty masks, and provides a 7-point pre-submission validation checklist to guarantee Kaggle acceptance.

4. Standalone Code Examples & Synthetic Walkthrough

To support hands-on learning without disclosing competition training results, this curriculum includes complete, self-contained educational resources built entirely on synthetic data:

1. Interactive Walkthrough Notebook

  • Jupyter Notebook: notebooks/synthetic_pipeline_walkthrough.ipynb
  • Paired Python Script: notebooks/synthetic_pipeline_walkthrough.py
  • Notebook Setup Guide: notebooks/README.md

This walkthrough runs on CPU in under 5 seconds and demonstrates every stage of the pipeline from synthetic strip generation to U-Net optimization and RLE encoding.

2. Standalone Diagnostic Scripts

  • Dataset & Batching Diagnostic: examples/toy_dataset_loader.py Validates __getitem__ tensor shapes, normalization, and DataLoader collation.

  • RLE Roundtrip Diagnostic: examples/toy_rle_roundtrip.py Verifies column-major RLE encoding, empty-mask single-space handling, and boundary clamping.

  • U-Net Optimization Lifecycle: examples/toy_unet_step.py Executes a forward pass, BCE loss calculation, backward pass, and optimizer step on a lightweight 2-level U-Net on CPU.

5. Teaching Philosophy and Boundaries

These study guides are designed as a guiding hand, not a shortcut:

  • They explain the underlying mathematics, tensor dimensions, software interfaces, failure modes, and debugging techniques.
  • They do not provide a copy-paste competition training script, pre-computed hyperparameter values, or trained model checkpoints.
  • They do not reveal confidential exploratory data analysis findings or performance benchmarks from the private training set.
  • You must write, audit, and debug your own segmentation pipeline against data/public/.
